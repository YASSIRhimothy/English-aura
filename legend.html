The Riddle of Sultan Wiil Waal

Long ago in the Horn of Africa, the legendary Sultan Wiil Waal ruled over his province. He was a highly intelligent leader, celebrated for his extraordinary wit, bravery, and use of riddles to govern and unite his people. However, he sometimes believed that he alone possessed true intellect. To see if there was anyone in the land who could match his mind, he called all the men of the region to his palace and issued a grand riddle.

"Bring me a part of one of your sheep," the Sultan commanded. "The part you choose must perfectly symbolize what can divide people, or what can unite them as one."

The men left the palace rubbing their chins in deep thought. Desperate to prove their wisdom to the Sultan, they each returned home to slaughter their finest sheep. Believing that the king would want something grand or delicious, the men selected the absolute prime cuts of meat. One man wrapped up a plump leg, another chose a tender shoulder, and others brought the ribs, liver, or heart.

In a distant corner of the province lived a very poor farmer who had many children and only a few sheep left to his name. Despondent, he realized he would have to slaughter his finest remaining animal just to participate in the Sultan's test. As he reluctantly prepared to butcher the sheep, he sighed heavily and muttered that he had absolutely no idea what the answer to Wiil Waal's riddle could be.

His oldest daughter overheard his despair. She looked at her father and told him not to worry because she knew the answer to the Sultan's riddle. She instructed him to bring the Sultan the gullet.

The farmer was deeply skeptical. The gullet and tongue piece was considered a worthless, scraps-only part of the animal—not a prized delicacy fit for royalty. Yet, seeing the absolute certainty in his daughter’s eyes, he trusted her. He packed up the lowly piece of meat and made the long trek back to the palace.

One by one, the wealthy and proud men of the province presented their fine, juicy cuts of meat to Wiil Waal. The Sultan shook his head at each one, growing increasingly bored and disappointed by their lack of insight.

Finally, the poor farmer stepped forward and nervously handed over the wrapped sheep's gullet. Wiil Waal’s eyes instantly lit up. He smiled and demanded to know why the farmer brought this specific part, asking him to explain its meaning.

The farmer, repeating the words of his clever daughter, replied: "Your Majesty, the gullet is the passage for speech and food. Through the words that pass through it, humans can speak kindness, negotiate peace, and unite as brothers. But through those same throats, humans can shout insults, tell lies, and declare wars that tear people apart. It is speech that either divides us or binds us together."

Sultan Wiil Waal was astonished by the depth of the answer. He declared that the riddle had finally been solved and demanded to know if the farmer had come up with the answer himself. The honest farmer confessed that it was his eldest daughter’s wisdom. Impressed by her remarkable intellect, Wiil Waal rewarded the family richly and eventually sought the clever daughter's hand in marriage, viewing her as his true equal in wit.

Setting

The historic towns, rolling plains, and pastoral lands of ancient Somalia.

Main Characters

Sultan Wiil Waal, a famous Somali ruler, and a clever young woman whose wisdom matches his own.

Main Themes

Wisdom, the power of speech, leadership, unity, and equality of intellect.
