The Legend of the Moonlit Camel

Long ago, near a small village in Somalia, there lived a young boy named Samatar. He was known for being curious and brave. His family raised camels, and Samatar spent many days helping his father take care of them.

One evening, Samatar noticed that one of their oldest camels had disappeared. The camel was called Hilaac, which means "lightning." Hilaac was a strange camel because he always seemed to know the safest path through the dry land.

Samatar decided to search for him. He followed a trail of footprints until the sun disappeared and the moon rose above the horizon.

As Samatar walked, he saw something shining in the distance. At first he thought it was a fire. When he came closer, he discovered Hilaac standing beside an ancient acacia tree.

Suddenly, the wind became very quiet. A mysterious voice came from the tree.

"The land remembers those who respect it."

Samatar looked around but could not see anyone. The voice continued, telling him that the village was going through a difficult time because the people had stopped caring for the land and had begun wasting the little water they had.

The mysterious voice told Samatar that beneath the acacia tree was an old hidden spring. However, it would only appear if the villagers worked together and promised to protect it.

Samatar returned to the village and told everyone what had happened. At first, nobody believed him. However, his father decided to follow him to the tree.

When the villagers arrived, they discovered a small spring hidden beneath the roots of the acacia tree. They worked together to protect the water and promised not to waste it.

From that day forward, the village became known for taking care of its land and water. Hilaac, the old camel, became a symbol of wisdom and was always treated with great respect.

Some people say that on nights when the moon is especially bright, a mysterious camel can still be seen walking beneath the acacia trees.
